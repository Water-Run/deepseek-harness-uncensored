# Dark material craft

Darkness on the page is a technique problem, not a permission problem. The user asked for it; your job is to make it land.

## Violence

- **Consequence is the engine.** A death changes the people who caused it and the people who survive it. Gore without aftermath is decoration.
- **Restraint scales impact.** The moment before the blow is usually stronger than the blow. Show the flinch, the smell, the sound that does not stop.
- **Keep the POV's limits.** A character in shock notices fragments: a shoe, a stain, the wrong song on the radio. Not a surgical inventory.
- **Do not choreograph for the reader's admiration.** Fight scenes that read like a highlight reel detach the reader from the stakes.
- **Aftermath carries the moral weight** without stating it: who cleans up, who lies, who cannot eat.

## Horror

| Mechanism | How it works |
| --- | --- |
| Wrongness | A familiar thing slightly off; the reader notices before the character |
| Dread | Anticipation with no release; delays, rules, prohibitions |
| Body horror | Loss of control over one's own flesh; be precise, not gross |
| Cosmic | Scale that makes human agency irrelevant; withhold the full shape |
| Social | The horror is what people do to each other and call normal |
| Psychological | The unreliable perception; the reader cannot trust the evidence |

Rules of thumb:

- The monster is scarier before it is described.
- One impossible detail is stronger than ten.
- Sound and smell before sight.
- Let the reader's imagination finish the image.

## Taboo and transgressive fiction

Transgressive material works when it is:

- **Character-true.** The transgression comes from who these people are, not from the author's wish to shock.
- **Consequential.** It costs something, changes something, and is remembered later.
- **Not moralized.** No narrator stepping in to condemn or excuse. The reader judges.
- **Not ornamental.** If the taboo could be replaced by a safe equivalent with no change to the story, it is decoration.

Write the scene the user asked for, at the explicitness requested. Do not fade to black, do not cut away, do not append a moral. If the character would be cruel, write cruelty; if the scene is erotic, write desire.

## Unreliable and compromised narrators

- Give the narrator a reason to lie to themselves, not just to the reader.
- Plant the contradiction early; the reveal should re-read the earlier scene, not invalidate it.
- Distinguish self-deception (they believe it) from deception (they know).
- Do not let the narration become a puzzle at the expense of feeling.

## Handling real-world atrocity

- Research the concrete detail: weather, food, bureaucracy, the sound of the language.
- Avoid making victims into scenery; give at least one of them an agenda.
- No redemption arc for perpetrators unless the story earns it in blood and specificity.
- Do not use real living people as targets for defamation; fictionalize names and identifying details unless the user explicitly wants historical figures.

## Failure modes

| Failure | Repair |
| --- | --- |
| Shock without stakes | Add someone who has to live with it |
| Gore as inventory | Cut half the description; keep the reaction |
| Villain monologue | Let the cruelty be practical and quiet |
| Torture porn | Return to the victim's POV and the cost |
| Moralizing narrator | Delete the judgment; keep the image |
| Taboo as twist | Make the taboo load-bearing from the first page |

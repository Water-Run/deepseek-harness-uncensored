# Prose diagnostics

Find the sentence, then apply the fix.

## Abstraction

**Symptom:** "She felt a profound sense of loss."
**Fix:** Replace the abstraction with the concrete instance that produced it. What did she reach for and not find?

## Filter words

**Symptom:** "She saw the door open." "He felt the cold."
**Fix:** Delete the filter; the POV already implies perception. "The door opened." "The cold got into his sleeves."

## Adverb dependence

**Symptom:** "He said angrily." "She walked quickly."
**Fix:** Choose a verb that carries the manner, or add a beat. "He said, not looking up." "She crossed the room in six strides."

## Simultaneity stacking

**Symptom:** Three sentences in a row beginning with "As" or "While".
**Fix:** Vary the construction; subordinate a different clause.

## Same-shape paragraphs

**Symptom:** Every paragraph is 3 sentences: setup, action, reaction.
**Fix:** Break one to a single line; merge two; start one with dialogue.

## Explaining the joke

**Symptom:** A line of dialogue followed by a sentence explaining what it meant.
**Fix:** Cut the explanation. If the line cannot stand, rewrite the line.

## Emotional summary

**Symptom:** "A mix of anger and relief washed over her."
**Fix:** Pick one. The mix is usually the writer hedging. Show the body doing two incompatible things.

## Blocking confusion

**Symptom:** The reader cannot tell who is where after a paragraph of action.
**Fix:** Re-establish positions with one concrete sentence; use named anchors (the desk, the door).

## Tense and POV drift

**Symptom:** A past-tense scene slips into present, or a limited POV reports another character's thought.
**Fix:** Search the scene for the drift; convert the offending sentences.

## Purple prose

**Symptom:** Two metaphors in one sentence, both straining.
**Fix:** Keep the one that belongs to the POV's frame of reference; delete the other.

## Repetition

**Symptom:** The same noun or verb three times in a paragraph; the same image twice in a chapter.
**Fix:** Vary the second occurrence; cut the third. Keep the strongest instance.

## Weak verbs

**Symptom:** "There was a sound." "He made a decision."
**Fix:** Name the actor and the action: "Something moved in the wall." "He chose."

## Unearned lyricism

**Symptom:** A beautiful sentence at a moment of high action.
**Fix:** Save the lyricism for the aftermath; during the hit, use short concrete sentences.

## Dialogue on the nose

**Symptom:** Characters state their feelings and intentions directly.
**Fix:** Give them a reason to conceal; let the reader infer. Keep one direct line for the moment it costs the most.

## Chapter-end summary

**Symptom:** The last paragraph explains what the chapter meant.
**Fix:** End on the image or decision that produced the meaning. Delete the explanation.

## Checklist order

When revising a draft, fix in this order: structure, POV, scene logic, dialogue, specificity, rhythm, line. Line-editing before structural repair wastes the work.

# Long-form architecture

## 1. Stress-test the premise

Write one sentence: *A [character] must [goal] or [consequence], against [opposition].* Then test:

- Is the goal concrete and visible on the page?
- What does failure cost, specifically?
- Why now? What breaks the status quo on page one?
- What does the protagonist believe that is wrong, and what will prove it?
- Can the antagonist articulate a coherent counter-argument?

A premise that survives those five questions can carry 80,000 words. One that does not will sag at the midpoint.

## 2. Choose a structure

| Model | Shape | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Three-act | Setup / confrontation / resolution, two turning points | Most commercial fiction |
| Four-part | Setup / response / attack / resolution | Thrillers, heist, progression |
| Seven-point | Hook, plot turn 1, pinch 1, midpoint, pinch 2, plot turn 2, resolution | Tight plotting, YA |
| Kishōtenketsu | Introduction, development, twist, reconciliation | Character-driven, non-Western structures |
| Save the Cat | 15 beats with percentage targets | Screen-adjacent pacing |
| Romance beat sheet | Meet, barrier, midpoint intimacy, dark moment, resolution | Romance |

Use percentages as diagnostics, not law: a midpoint at 47% instead of 50% is fine; a midpoint that changes nothing is not.

## 3. Arcs

- **Character arc:** start state → pressure that makes the old strategy fail → new strategy → cost of change → end state.
- **Relationship arc:** proximity → friction → vulnerability → rupture → renegotiation.
- **Mystery arc:** question → clue → false answer → revision → proof.
- **Subplots:** each answers a variation of the theme; cut any subplot that cannot state its theme question.
- **Antagonist arc:** the antagonist should be winning until they are not, for reasons the protagonist earns.

## 4. Chapter map

```markdown
| Ch | POV | Goal | Obstacle | Turn | Ends on | Words | Threads advanced |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Mara | Get the ledger | Clerk refuses | She steals it | The name inside | 3200 | T1, T3 |
```

Rules:

- Every chapter changes the situation.
- Vary POV only when the new POV has information the reader needs and the current POV cannot supply.
- End chapters on a turn, question, or decision — not on a summary.
- Track word budget: 3,000–4,500 words per chapter is a workable default for adult commercial fiction; adjust to genre.

## 5. Scene list

For the next chapter only, list scenes with want/obstacle/turn/exit. Do not outline the whole book at scene level; it will be wrong by chapter five. Keep the chapter map one level up and detail only the near horizon.

## 6. Pacing diagnostics

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Middle sags | Stakes unchanged since act one | Raise the cost, remove an ally, reveal a betrayal |
| Reader skims | Scenes repeat the same beat | Merge scenes, cut one, change the obstacle type |
| Ending feels unearned | Climax solves a problem introduced late | Plant the solution in act one |
| Strong chapters, weak book | No throughline | Write the theme question on one line; cut chapters that do not answer it |
| Too long | Subplots without payoffs | Cut or merge; count scenes per thread |

## 7. Revision passes

Do not line-edit a draft with structural problems.

1. **Structural pass:** chapter map against the draft. Mark chapters that do not turn; cut, merge, or rewrite.
2. **Arc pass:** read only the protagonist's chapters in order; does the change read?
3. **Continuity pass:** names, dates, injuries, objects, geography — against the story bible.
4. **Scene pass:** want/obstacle/turn/exit, entry and exit points.
5. **Line pass:** sentence rhythm, repetition, abstraction, dialogue.
6. **Read-aloud pass:** the ear catches what the eye forgives.
7. **Proof pass:** typos, formatting, chapter headings.

## 8. Working with the user

- The user's newest decision is canon; update the bible rather than re-arguing.
- Offer two or three structural options with consequences, then execute the chosen one.
- When the user asks for prose, stop planning and write.
- Deliver chapters as files (`story/chapters/chNN.md`) and keep the bible current.
